01 — History

A brief, honest timeline of the Union

Two and a half centuries of expansion, conflict, reform and reinvention — condensed into the moments that still shape American life today.

Download-style fact sheet ↓
1776 · INDEPENDENCE

Declaration of Independence

Thirteen British colonies declare independence on July 4. The Revolutionary War (1775–1783) secures sovereignty with French support.

1787–1789 · CONSTITUTION

A new republic is designed

Constitutional Convention in Philadelphia creates a federal system with checks and balances. Bill of Rights ratified 1791.

1803 · EXPANSION

Louisiana Purchase doubles the nation

Jefferson buys 828,000 sq miles from France. Lewis & Clark expedition maps the West (1804–1806).

1861–1865 · CIVIL WAR

Union preserved, slavery abolished

Civil war over slavery and union. Emancipation Proclamation (1863) and 13th Amendment (1865) end legal slavery.

1920–1945 · WORLD POWER

Suffrage, Depression, world wars

19th Amendment grants women’s suffrage (1920). Great Depression → New Deal. U.S. emerges from WWII as a superpower.

1960s · CIVIL RIGHTS

Equality under law advances

Civil Rights Act (1964), Voting Rights Act (1965), moon landing (1969). A decade of protest and progress.

1991–2026 · MODERN ERA

Innovation age & 250th birthday

Cold War ends, internet economy booms. America marks its Semiquincentennial in 2026 — 250 years since 1776.

Editor’s note

Why 1776 still matters

“We hold these truths to be self-evident, that all men are created equal…” The promise was imperfect at birth — but it created a standard every generation has been asked to live up to.

02 — Geography

A continent-sized country

Arctic tundra to tropical islands, deserts to temperate rainforest — the U.S. spans six time zones and nearly every climate on Earth.

▲

Mountains & Plains

Rockies and Appalachians frame the Great Plains. Denali (6,190 m) in Alaska is North America’s highest peak.

≈

Rivers & Lakes

Mississippi–Missouri (5,970 km combined) drains the heartland. The Great Lakes hold ~21% of world surface fresh water.

◐

Mediterranean California, humid subtropical Southeast, arid Southwest, subarctic Alaska, tropical Hawaii & Florida.

03 — Government & Politics

A federal republic of checks and balances

Power is divided between the national government and 50 states — and within Washington, across three co-equal branches.

◩

Legislative — Congress

Makes federal law. Bicameral: 100 Senators (2 per state, 6-yr terms) + 435 Representatives (by population, 2-yr terms).

  • Senate confirms judges & treaties
  • House originates revenue bills
  • Capitol Building, Washington D.C.
⬣

Executive — President

Enforces law, commands armed forces, conducts foreign policy. Elected every 4 years via the Electoral College (538 votes; 270 to win). Max two terms.

  • White House + 15 executive departments
  • Vice President presides over Senate
  • Issues executive orders
⬔

Judicial — Supreme Court

Interprets the Constitution. Nine justices with lifetime appointments. Judicial review since Marbury v. Madison (1803).

  • Federal courts + 13 circuit courts
  • Landmark rulings shape civil rights
  • Meets in the Supreme Court Building
HOW ELECTIONS WORK

Every vote, every level

Federal, state and local elections run on fixed cycles. Most states use primaries + general election in November.

Presidential · every 4 yrs
Next after 2024: 2028. Midterms (House + ⅓ Senate) every 2 years in between.
Two-party system + more
Democrats & Republicans dominate, with independents, Libertarian and Green parties also on ballots.
